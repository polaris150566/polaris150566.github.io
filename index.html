I am an undergraduate at the University of Science and Technology of China, majoring in Biosciences. I am currently a research intern with Prof. Yu Li at CUHK, and I work with Prof. Haiyan Liu and Prof. Quan Chen at the MOE Key Laboratory for Membraneless Organelles and Cellular Dynamics at USTC.

My research is on deep generative models for protein and RNA design, centered on the inverse folding problem: given a 3D backbone, which sequence will actually fold into it and function in its native environment? I am particularly interested in membrane proteins and GPCRs — cases where models trained on soluble proteins break down, because the backbone they are handed is simply out of distribution.

I am preparing to apply for a Ph.D. in machine learning for structural biology.

research

May 2026 – present CUHK

RNA sequence design with discrete diffusion

Research Intern · PI: Prof. Yu Li

Building a generative model from scratch for RNA sequence design conditioned on 3D backbone structures. Replaced conventional autoregressive generation with a discrete diffusion framework, so the sequence is refined iteratively rather than committed to left-to-right. Added atomic-level conditioning to improve design accuracy at RNA–protein complex interfaces.

Sep. 2025 – present USTC

Statistical energy functions for protein design evaluation

Co-supervised by Prof. Haiyan Liu & Prof. Quan Chen

Developing a Mixture Density Network to predict statistical energy functions for screening de novo protein designs, with an RNN predicting pairwise residue energies autoregressively. Built the data pipeline: processed SAbDab and PPI structural datasets, regenerated side-chain conformations with ABACUS-T-Ab and ProteinMPNN, used AlphaFold 3 for decoy generation, and encoded pre- and post-redesign structures into pairwise features as positive and negative samples.

Apr. 2025 – May 2026 USTC

ABACUS-T-Mem: membrane protein sequence design

Independent Project · National College Students' Innovation Program

Mainstream inverse-folding models are trained almost entirely on soluble proteins, so on a membrane protein backbone they tend to generate hydrophilic sequences in the transmembrane region. I fine-tuned ABACUS-T to close that distribution gap. Built a training set from PDBTM using TMDET 4.0 for membrane-position annotation, encoded membrane depth via radial basis functions, and reweighted the loss toward transmembrane structural features.

On an independent test set of 129 membrane proteins, sequence recovery improved from 37.87% to 40.01% (Wilcoxon signed-rank p < 0.001). The largest gain was at the membrane–water interface, 26.7% → 30.5% — the model learned membrane-specific rules rather than gaining points uniformly. Designed sequences shifted toward native composition, with charged residues depleted and hydrophobic residues enriched at the interface, while aqueous-exposed regions kept their polar residues.

I also compared several strategies for injecting membrane-position information: background prior injection, W/Y loss weighting, classifier-free guidance, ControlNet-style side branches, adaLN, and cross-attention. Most did not help, and the reason turned out to be more interesting than a win would have been. Membrane depth is a weak, noisy, continuous geometric signal rather than a strong semantic label, so the gap between conditional and unconditional predictions is too small to steer generation reliably. Parameter-change analysis showed the ESM activation weights moving the most — the model was compensating for the injected signal as if it were noise. That pushed the work toward low-degree-of-freedom gating at the output logits instead.

Mar. – Oct. 2024 iGEM

Tumor angiogenesis inhibition via surface-displayed VEGF antibodies

Team Leader · USTC 2024 iGEM

Engineered Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 to display VEGF-binding proteins and inhibit tumor angiogenesis. Screened four surface-display systems using mCherry fusions and confocal microscopy, validated VEGF-binding by ELISA, and designed an MMP-responsive masking peptide so the binding protein activates only inside the tumor microenvironment. Built a finite element model in Python for antigen-antibody interaction kinetics; predictions matched the ELISA results.
