Discrete Diffusion

RNA sequence design with discrete diffusion

A generative model built from scratch for RNA sequence design conditioned on 3D backbone structures.

May 2026 – present CUHK Research Intern PI: Prof. Yu Li

The problem

Inverse folding for RNA asks the same question as it does for proteins: given a three-dimensional backbone, which sequence will actually fold into it? RNA makes this harder than proteins in one specific way — with only four nucleotides, the sequence space is narrow but the structural consequences of each choice are severe, and the same fold can be reached by very different sequences.

Approach

Most sequence design models generate autoregressively, committing to each position left-to-right. Once an early nucleotide is chosen, later positions have to work around it, and the model has no way to revisit that decision.

I replaced that with a discrete diffusion framework. The sequence starts fully masked and is denoised over multiple passes, so every position can be revised in light of the others. This turns design into iterative refinement rather than a single irreversible sweep, which suits a problem where nucleotide choices are strongly coupled.

Atomic-level conditioning

RNA rarely acts alone. Much of its function happens at interfaces with proteins, where individual atom positions — not just backbone geometry — determine which nucleotide fits. I added atomic-level conditioning so the model sees this finer structural context, which improved design accuracy specifically at RNA–protein complex interfaces.

Status

Ongoing. The model is built and training; benchmarking and wet-lab validation are in progress.
